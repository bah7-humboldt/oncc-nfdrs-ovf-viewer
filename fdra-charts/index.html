How to interpret these charts

The x-axis is day-of-year (Feb 29 is folded into Feb 28 in leap years so every year lines up on the same axis, same convention scripts/FDRACharts.py's own static NFDRS Fire Danger Charts use). The colored bands are percentile thresholds computed from the same historical window as the average/extreme lines: 60th/80th/90th/97th/99.99th percentile for most indices, or the same tiers against the LOW tail for the two fuel moisture indices (dry = dangerous there, so low values shade toward the most severe color instead of high ones).

Historical average/extreme: the gray line is the mean value for that calendar day across every year in the historical window; the red line is that same day's most extreme (driest, or highest danger) value ever recorded.

Severe Fire Danger Index (SFDI): Jolly et al. 2019 (Fire 2(3):47). Each day's ERC and BI are converted to percentiles of this station's (or PSA's/GACC's) own history and multiplied (p = ERC′ × BI′). SFDI is that product's percentile within the same history, so it runs 0–100 and rises only when ERC and BI are high together. The classes are the paper's: Low ≤60, Moderate 60–80, High 80–90, Very High 90–97, Severe >97. They match this toolkit's PSA danger maps. A PSA's SFDI comes from its averaged ERC and BI, not from averaging its stations' SFDI.

Period of record: the dates printed above each chart are the first and last day that station (or PSA/GACC rollup) actually has data. Many RAWS were installed after the climatology window's start, so their average, extreme, percentile bands and fire dots cover only those years.

Current year / forecast: the blue line is this year's actual observed daily value through the most recent FEMS observation; the green line is FEMS's current 7-day forecast, picking up where the observed line leaves off.

Year: defaults to the current year (observed + forecast). Picking a past year swaps in that year's observed daily values as a purple dashed line (no forecast) against the same climatology, e.g. to compare this season with 2020 or 2021. A station has no line for years before its period of record.

Historical fires: each dot is a fire, placed at its discovery date and at the index value actually observed that day at this station (or averaged across the PSA/GACC for a rollup chart). It is not placed on the average line, so a dot's height shows how the fire's start day compared with climatology. Dot size is fire size class; fires discovered the same day share one dot. Only fires inside the climatology window can plot, since each dot needs an observation on its date. Station charts show every fire in that station's PSA. Hover a dot for the fire name(s), acreage and percentile.

Fire cause: a fire's popup lists its NWCG cause (PMS 910) from broad to narrow: Cause Classification, General Cause, Specific Cause, Specific Cause Detail. For example, "Human, Equipment and Vehicle Use, Trailer, Brakes". Levels with no value (or "Unknown") are left off. FPA FOD records (1992 through the edition's last year) carry only the first two levels; the specific levels come from InFORM FODR records (later years). An Undetermined fire shows only its investigation status when one is recorded, e.g. "Undetermined, Under investigation". The cause filter can also select by FireFamily Plus statistical cause (1 Lightning through 9 Miscellaneous), the code FireFamily Plus analyses use. It is less exact: code 2 covers Equipment and Vehicle Use and Power Generation/Transmission/Distribution, and code 9 covers Undetermined, Firearms and Explosives Use, Fireworks, and Other Causes.
